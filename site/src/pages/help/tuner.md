# Use the tuner

Crosstune has a tuner for any instrument, from upright bass to fiddle. Play a note, and the tuner shows the note it hears and how far that note is from in tune. The tuner is free on every plan.

## Open the tuner

Choose **Tuner** in the tab bar or the sidebar. Tuner is next to Recordings on every device.

The first time you open the tuner, Crosstune asks to use your microphone. Allow it, because the tuner cannot hear you without it. If you did not allow it, turn on the microphone for Crosstune in the settings of your phone, tablet, or computer. In a web browser, allow the microphone for the Crosstune site in the browser settings. Then open the tuner again.

## When the tuner listens

The tuner listens only while it is on the screen. It stops listening in these cases:

- You go to another screen.
- You play a tune or a recording. The tuner listens again when you pause.
- You start a recording. The recording uses the microphone.

When you open the tuner, a recording or an Apple Music song that is playing pauses. A YouTube, Spotify, or Bandcamp link that is playing closes.

## Read the tuner

Play one note and let it ring. The tuner shows these things:

- The note that it hears, with the octave as a small number beside the letter. For example, A4 is the A above middle C, which is the A string of a fiddle.
- How far the note is from in tune, in cents. A cent is one hundredth of a half step. A minus sign means that the note is too low, or flat. A plus sign means that the note is too high, or sharp.
- The word Flat or Sharp under the cents, when the note is more than 3 cents from in tune.
- A bar at each side of the screen. When the note is in tune, the bars are level with the note. When the note is flat, the bars move down. When the note is sharp, the bars move up.

Behind each bar is a box. The box marks the area from 10 cents flat to 10 cents sharp. Turn the tuning peg until the bars are in the boxes and level with the note.

The color of the bars shows how close the note is:

- More than 15 cents from in tune: orange.
- From 10 to 15 cents: light orange.
- In the boxes: the bars change slowly from light orange to green as you get closer, and the screen starts to turn green.
- 3 cents or less: green. The note is in tune.

When you stop playing, the last note stays on the screen until the tuner hears a new note. When the tuner stops listening, the screen clears and shows "Play a note" again.

## Change the reference pitch

The tuner measures every note against A4 = 440 Hz, the standard most folk musicians tune to. If your group tunes to a different A, you can set any value from 430 to 450 Hz. The place to change it depends on your device:

- On a phone, tap the button with your initials at the top of the screen, then choose **Tuner**.
- On a tablet or in a web browser, open **Settings**, then choose **Tuner**.
- On a Mac, open **Crosstune > Settings**, then choose **General**.

The reference pitch syncs to your other devices. When the reference pitch is not 440 Hz, the tuner shows it under the title, for example A4 = 442 Hz. This way, you always know that it is set.

## Tips for upright bass

Phone and laptop microphones do not hear the low strings of an upright bass well. To get a steady reading, do these things:

- Hold the phone near the bridge, or put it on a stand near the instrument.
- Pluck the string firmly and let it ring.
- Play in a quiet place. Other instruments near you can make the low notes difficult to hear.

If the tuner shows nothing for the lowest string, tune its harmonic instead. Touch the string lightly at its middle point and pluck it. The tuner shows the same note, one octave higher. When the harmonic is in tune, the open string is in tune.
