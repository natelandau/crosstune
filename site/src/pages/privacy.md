# Privacy policy

Last updated: 2026-10-18

Crosstune is run by Nathaniel Landau, an individual, from New York in the United States. In this policy, "we" and "us" mean Nathaniel Landau as the operator of Crosstune. Questions and requests go to [support@crosstune.app](mailto:support@crosstune.app), or by post to 367 St Marks Ave #1240, Brooklyn, NY 11238, United States.

This policy covers this site (crosstune.app), the web app (my.crosstune.app), and the Crosstune apps for iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

## The short version

- Your catalog is private to you. No other user can see it.
- We use your data to run Crosstune for you. There are no ads, we do not sell your data, and we do not use it to train AI models.
- The apps send usage analytics to PostHog, never your content. You can turn this off with Share usage data. In the European Union and the rest of the European Economic Area (Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway), it starts off. It also starts off when the app cannot tell which country you are in.
- You can export everything you made and delete your account from Settings at any time.

## Who is responsible for your data

Nathaniel Landau is the controller of the personal data described here, which means he decides how and why it is processed. Contact details are at the top of this page.

## What we collect

### Your account

- Your email address. Clerk holds it for sign-in, and we keep a copy in our database so we can contact you about your account.
- If you sign in with Google or Apple, the name and profile details they share. If you use Apple's Hide My Email, we receive a private relay address instead of your real one.
- When you created your account, when a device last synced it, and your plan, trial, and subscription status.

### Your catalog

Everything you add to your catalog, which can include:

- Tunes and their details: titles and other titles, key, mode, tunings, genre, type, time signature, part structure, composer, and lyrics.
- Your relationship to each tune: its status, who or where you learned it from, when, and your notes. What you write here can name other people, such as a teacher.
- Lists, links you paste, and the title we fetch for each link.
- Your settings, such as the instruments you play and the music services you search.

### Your recordings

The audio you record, upload, or save from a link, with its label, notes, players, when it was added, when and where it was recorded, practice loops, and trims. When you upload an audio file, we keep the original file as you uploaded it, including any information stored inside it, such as a title or the device that made it.

Where a recording was made is a precise location: its coordinates, and, for a recording made in the app, how accurate they are. We keep one when:

- You record in the web app or the iPhone, iPad, or Mac app and allow Crosstune to use your location. If you turn off precise location for Crosstune on your device, we keep the approximate location your device gives instead.
- You upload an audio file that already holds the place it was recorded, and the recording has no place yet. In the same way, we use the time stored in the file if the recording has no recorded date yet.

If you do not allow location, a recording you make has no place, and we never work one out another way, such as from your IP address. We never use your device's location for an upload. You can set, change, or clear a recording's place at any time.

### Your scans

The images you add to a tune from the camera, your photo library, or a file, such as written music, lyric sheets, or notes. Before an image leaves your device, the app removes its embedded details, such as location and camera data.

### Your activity

When you play recordings and links, how long you listen, your practice sessions, when you view a tune's scans, and changes to a tune's status. This history powers your stats.

### Purchases

If you buy Premium, we receive the plan you bought, when it renews or ends, and where you bought it. We never receive your card number. See Who processes it.

### Messages to us

If you email us, we keep the message and our reply.

### Technical data

Every request to our servers carries your IP address and basic details about your device and app version. Our servers keep short-lived logs of these requests, without the text of your searches. See How long we keep it.

When a device syncs, we work out the country it is connecting from, using its IP address, and keep that country with your account. We keep only the latest country, never the IP address. We use it to count how many people use Crosstune in each country. Country lookups use [IP geolocation by DB-IP](https://db-ip.com), licensed under CC BY 4.0.

### Usage analytics

See Usage analytics below.

## Why we use it

The law in some places, such as the European Union and the United Kingdom, asks us to name the legal reason for each use. Ours are:

- To provide Crosstune: your account, sync between your devices, storage of your catalog, recordings, and scans, export, and your stats. We need this data to perform our agreement with you.
- To sell and manage Premium: purchases, renewals, refunds, and records the law requires us to keep. This performs our agreement with you and meets our legal obligations.
- To keep Crosstune secure and working: logs, error reports, fraud and abuse prevention. This is in our legitimate interest in running a reliable and safe service, and we keep it to what that needs.
- To contact you about your account: sign-in codes, warnings before we delete stored files, and changes to these terms or this policy. This performs our agreement with you.
- To count how many people use Crosstune, and how many use it actively, in each country, from the country we keep with your account. This is in our legitimate interest in knowing where Crosstune is used, and it never leaves our servers.
- To understand how Crosstune is used and improve it: usage analytics. In the European Union and the rest of the European Economic Area (Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway), we rely on your consent, which you give by turning on Share usage data and can withdraw by turning it off. Elsewhere, we rely on our legitimate interest in improving Crosstune, and you can object at any time by turning off Share usage data.
- To meet legal obligations, such as answering lawful requests and keeping tax records.

Your email address is required to create an account. Everything else in your catalog is up to you. We do not make decisions about you by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects.

## Who processes it

We use these service providers to run Crosstune. Each one processes your data for us, under a contract that limits what it can do with the data and requires it to protect the data at least as well as this policy does.

- Clerk handles sign-in. It holds your email address, your name and profile details, and your session data, which can include your IP address and device. To keep you signed in to the web app at my.crosstune.app, it sets cookies on the crosstune.app domain. Its sign-in form can load a bot check from Cloudflare.
- Neon hosts the database that holds your account, your catalog, and your activity.
- Railway runs the Crosstune API.
- Cloudflare serves this site and the web app, passes the web app's requests to the API, and stores your recordings and scans in its R2 storage.
- Fastmail receives email sent to support@crosstune.app.
- Resend sends our emails about your account and your Premium subscription: purchase confirmations, yearly renewal reminders, refund confirmations, notices of significant changes to these terms or this policy, and the warnings before Crosstune deletes the recordings and scans of an account that is not on Premium and has not synced for a year. It receives your email address and the content of those emails.
- Sentry receives error reports from the API and the web app. We set Sentry to leave out the content of requests, your search text, your email address, your IP address, and cookies.
- PostHog Cloud, in the United States, receives usage analytics from this site, the web app, and the iPhone, iPad, and Mac apps. See Usage analytics below.
- RevenueCat keeps the record of your Premium subscription from Apple and Link. It holds your Crosstune account ID, what you bought, and when it renews or ends. Its code in the apps also sends it basic device and store details.

These companies receive data as independent companies under their own privacy policies, not on our behalf:

- Apple sells Premium in the App Store. Apple holds your payment details, and Crosstune never receives them. If you sign in with Apple, Apple confirms who you are to us. See [Apple's privacy policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).
- Google confirms who you are to us if you sign in with Google. See [Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).
- Link, a service of Stripe, takes the payment for Premium on the web. Link holds your name, email address, payment details, and billing address. Crosstune never receives your card number. See [Link's privacy policy](https://link.com/privacy).

### Music services and links

When you use these features, the service you use receives what that feature needs:

- Find recordings sends your search text from our server to Apple Music, TIDAL, or the Internet Archive. Our server's address is sent, not yours. For other services, the app opens that service's own search page with your search text, and the service sees your device as any visitor.
- When you paste a link, our server fetches the page to read its title. The site sees our server, not you.
- When you save audio from a Slippery-Hill link, our server downloads the file from Slippery-Hill into your storage.
- When a link plays in the app, the player comes from that service, such as YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music, TIDAL, SoundCloud, Bandcamp, or the Internet Archive. It loads from your device, and that service can see your IP address and set its own cookies under its own privacy policy. YouTube players use YouTube's API Services, so [Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) applies to them.
- On iPhone, iPad, and Mac, Apple Music plays through your own Apple Account.

### Apple Maps

Apple Maps names the places where your recordings were made and finds the places you search for:

- To name a recording's place, our server sends its coordinates to Apple Maps. Our server's address is sent, not yours, and nothing else about you or the recording.
- When you search for a place in the web app, our server sends your search text to Apple Maps in the same way. In the iPhone, iPad, and Mac apps, the search goes from your device to Apple Maps, under [Apple's privacy policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).

### AI providers

Some features can send content you choose, such as an image, to a third-party AI provider to process it for you. Before a feature does this for the first time, it tells you what it sends and to whom, and asks your permission. You can turn it off in Settings. We only use AI providers whose contracts forbid them from using your content to train their models, and we name each one in this policy before any feature uses it. A user under 18 needs a parent or guardian's agreement to turn on an AI feature.

### Other people

Today, no other Crosstune user can see your catalog. If we add ways to share tunes, lists, or recordings, or to contribute to a shared catalog, nothing you have will be shared unless you choose to share it, and we will update this policy before that happens.

We may also disclose data if the law requires it, to protect someone's safety, or to enforce our terms. If Crosstune moves to a company that Nathaniel Landau controls, or is sold, your data moves with it under this policy, and we will tell you before that happens.

## Data on your devices

The app stores your full catalog on your own devices so that it works offline. When you sign out, the app deletes that copy from the device. If some of your changes or scans have not uploaded yet, the app does not sign out until they have, so that nothing is lost. Your appearance and similar device preferences stay on the device. On iPhone and iPad, a new recording can be part of your device backup until it uploads.

## Usage analytics

This site, the web app, and the iPhone, iPad, and Mac apps send usage analytics to PostHog.

PostHog receives each request's IP address and uses it to estimate your approximate location, which is your country and city. It also receives which pages and features you use, and your device and software details. On this site, those details are your browser, operating system, screen size, time zone, and the page that referred you. In the web app, they are your browser, operating system, screen size, language, time zone, and the app version, and whether you use a phone, tablet, or computer and run the app installed or in a browser tab. In the iPhone, iPad, and Mac apps, they are your device model, operating system version, screen size, language, time zone, whether you are on Wi-Fi or cellular, and the app version. The apps also report whether assistive features, such as VoiceOver, are on. They never report what those features read. The apps also report when you install, update, open, and leave them.

The web app and the apps also report counts and choices that describe your use without its content: when you signed up, your catalog size and storage used in rough ranges, which tune details you fill in, your settings, and when you view Premium, start a purchase, or complete one.

On this site, visits are counted without cookies, and the analytics are not linked to an account. When you click a Create account button, the site records which button you clicked, with the page that referred you and any campaign tags from the link. That record is not linked to the account you create. The site also records your clicks and how fast its pages load. The site has no switch to turn them off, but a content blocker stops them.

The web app and the apps link this data to your account. They never send your email address or name to PostHog. Events can carry the internal IDs of tunes, lists, recordings, links, and scans. They never send tune names, notes, lyrics, scans, or recordings. They never send where a recording was made, its place name, or what you search for to find a place. They report only that you set or cleared a place, whether it came from a search or a suggestion, and whether it replaced one.

The apps do not record your screen or your sessions.

In the European Union and the rest of the European Economic Area (Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway), Share usage data is off until you turn it on, and the app sends nothing to PostHog until then. While the app cannot tell which country you are in, such as when it first opens without a connection, Share usage data also stays off and the app sends nothing. Everywhere else, Share usage data is on when you first use the app. To turn this off, turn off Share usage data in the app: Settings > About on the web, iPhone, and iPad, Settings > General on Mac. While it is off, the app stops collecting usage data. Data collected just before you turned it off can still upload.

When you delete your account, we ask PostHog to delete the analytics data linked to your account. We ask again over the following week to catch data sent just before the deletion. PostHog deletes events in a weekly batch. If Share usage data is on, the app then reports that an account was deleted, without anything that links the report to you.

## Cookies and device storage

This site sets no cookies. The web app uses:

- Clerk's cookies, which keep you signed in. They are needed for the app to work.
- Your browser's storage for your offline catalog, your preferences, and, while Share usage data is on, PostHog's state.
- Stripe's cookies during checkout, which help prevent fraud.
- Cookies or storage set by a music service's player while it plays.

The iPhone, iPad, and Mac apps keep your sign-in in the device keychain and your catalog and preferences on the device.

We do not respond to browser Do Not Track signals, because there is no common standard for them. We do not track you across other companies' sites or apps.

## What we do not do

There are no ads. We do not sell your data, or share it for advertising. We do not track you across other companies' apps and sites. We do not use your content to train AI models.

## How long we keep it

- Your account, catalog, recordings, and scans: until you delete them or your account, except as described in Deleting your data and Inactive accounts.
- Your activity history: until you delete your account.
- The country we keep with your account: until you delete your account. Each sync replaces it with the latest one.
- Database backups: deleted data can remain in our database's history for up to 30 days before it expires.
- Server logs: up to 30 days.
- Error reports in Sentry: up to 90 days.
- Usage analytics in PostHog: until you delete your account, and never longer than 7 years.
- Email to support: up to 3 years after the conversation ends.
- Purchase records: as long as tax and accounting law requires. Apple and Link keep their own records under their own policies.
- Refund records: when we refund a web subscription, because you withdrew or deleted your account, we keep a record of the refund confirmation we emailed you: the subscription's ID and when we sent it. We keep it even after you delete your account, to meet our legal and tax obligations.
- After you delete your account, we keep your account's internal ID so that the deleted account cannot sync again, and the refund records above. We keep no other data.

## Deleting your data

### Deleting items

When you delete a tune, list, link, recording, or scan, we delete its content and files. We keep a small marker, with no content, so the deletion reaches all of your devices.

### Deleting your account

Open Settings in the app and delete your account. This deletes your account, your catalog, your recordings and scans, and your activity from Crosstune, and the deletion cannot be undone. Export first if you want a copy.

- Deleting your account cancels a web subscription at once. It does not cancel an App Store subscription, so cancel that in your Apple Account first.
- If you signed in with Apple, we tell Apple to revoke Crosstune's access.
- Copies can remain in database backups, logs, and error reports until they expire on the schedule above.
- Link and Apple keep their own records of your purchases, and we keep the record of any refund, as described in How long we keep it.
- If our payment provider cannot be reached to cancel a web subscription, deletion waits, and the app asks you to try again later.

## Storage limit

Premium accounts hold 3 GB of recordings and scans together, and a trial holds 100 MB. Free accounts cannot add recordings or scans.

## Inactive accounts

If an account is not on Premium and no device syncs it for 12 months, we delete its recordings and scans. We send two emails before we delete them, each with a link to export your data. A sync from any device stops the deletion. Your account, tunes, and lists stay.

## Your rights

Wherever you live, you can:

- See and get a copy of your data. Export in Settings gives you your tunes, lists, recordings, and scans. For anything else, such as your activity history, email us.
- Correct your data, in the app or by asking us.
- Delete your data, in the app or by asking us.
- Object to or limit how we use your data, and withdraw consent you gave, such as with Share usage data.

Email [support@crosstune.app](mailto:support@crosstune.app) with your request from the email address on your account, so we know it is you. We answer within one month. If we refuse a request, we explain why, and you can ask us to reconsider by replying.

If you live in the European Union or the United Kingdom, you can also complain to your data protection authority. In the United Kingdom, that is the Information Commissioner's Office. If you live in a US state with a privacy law, you have the rights that law gives you, and you can appeal our answer to a request by replying to it.

## Children and teens

Crosstune is not for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. If you are a parent and believe your child under 13 has an account, email us and we will delete it.

Users from 13 to 17 need a parent or guardian to agree to our terms. A parent or guardian can ask us to see or delete their teen's data, or turn off Share usage data in their teen's app.

## Where your data is processed

We are in the United States, and our service providers store and process your data in the United States. Fastmail is an Australian company. If you are outside the United States, your data is transferred there. For transfers from the European Union, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland, our providers rely on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and its UK and Swiss extensions, or on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses. You can ask us for a copy of these safeguards.

## Security

Data travels between your devices and our servers over encrypted connections. Each account can reach only its own data. We limit each service provider's access to what it needs, and we remove embedded details from scans before they leave your device. No service is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your data, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

## Changes

If we make a significant change to this policy, we tell you by email or in the app before it takes effect, and we update the date at the top of this page. We will not use data we already have in a new way that needs your consent, such as sharing it with an AI provider, without asking you first.
